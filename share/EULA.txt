4. Updates and your data
------------------------

The app checks for a new version by downloading one small file,
https://shellnaga.sh/downloads/latest.toml (on Linux,
https://shellnaga.sh/downloads/linux/latest.toml), at most once a day. The
request carries the app's version and, like any connection, your IP address.
You can turn this off in Settings › App. A new version is downloaded only when
you click Update now, and its signature is checked before it is installed. A
font from Settings › Appearance › More fonts is downloaded from
https://shellnaga.sh/downloads/fonts/ only when you click Install, with the
same version and IP address, and stays under its own license, published next
to it. Besides that, the app sends only anonymous usage statistics, at the
level you choose in Settings › App › Privacy. At All statistics (the default),
it sends a count when it is installed and at most one a day when you open it,
each with a random ID, its version, your operating system version, processor
type, language and install type; the daily count also says the version it was
updated from and whether that update worked, how many times it closed
unexpectedly after opening (0, 1, or 2 or more), and, in ranges, how long its
window was in front (not while the PC is locked or asleep) and how many times
it was opened on the last day you used it, and, also in ranges, on how many of
the 7 days before you opened it, how much memory the PC has and who makes its
graphics card (never the model). At Errors only, the same install and daily
counts still go, with the same random ID and details, the update results and
the unexpected closes, but without the time, the openings, the days of use and
the PC's memory and graphics. Off, or the DisableUsageStats policy, sends
nothing. It sends nothing else about you or your use. How the website treats
visits is in the privacy policy (https://shellnaga.sh/privacy). Whatever you
type, connect to or store with Shell'Naga stays on your device.

If you turn on the local MCP server (off by default), AI assistants of your
Windows account on the same PC can read your tabs once you allow it and type
in them each time you confirm. It never listens to the network. You are
responsible for the assistants you connect and for what they do with what they
read.

If you turn on the AI assistant (off by default), Shell'Naga sends what you
choose to send (a command, its output, your question or attached text, after
hiding what looks like a secret) directly to the AI provider you configure,
using your own account or a model on your own PC. That provider's terms and
privacy policy govern what it does with it, and you are responsible for your
use of that provider and its costs. AI answers can be wrong: Shell'Naga never
runs a suggested command, and you decide whether to insert it and run it. The
cost estimates and token limits in the app are approximations for your
convenience, not a bill.
